# Product

## Register

product

## Users

ApplyTrack is for job seekers who are actively managing applications, interviews, follow-ups, offers, and rejections. They use it during a focused job search from desktop and mobile browsers, often returning quickly to update a status, add an application, or check progress.

## Product Purpose

ApplyTrack centralizes the job search pipeline so users can see what they applied to, what needs follow-up, and how their search is progressing. Success means users can add, find, update, import, and review applications without losing track of next actions.

## Brand Personality

Focused, practical, and encouraging. The product should feel like a capable workspace: calm enough for repeated use, structured enough for scanning, and warm enough to make a stressful job search feel manageable.

## Anti-references

Avoid marketing-site polish that gets in the way of the task, decorative controls that do not explain their function, cluttered spreadsheet-like density on small screens, and mobile layouts that simply shrink the desktop dashboard.

## Design Principles

- Keep the next action visible.
- Make dense application data scannable before making it decorative.
- Use standard product affordances for forms, navigation, sorting, filtering, and destructive actions.
- Adapt structure across screen sizes instead of just scaling typography.
- Preserve user trust around private job-search data and account recovery.

## Current Visual Direction

The current UI direction is documented in `DESIGN.md`. ApplyTrack uses a light professional workspace with a fixed desktop sidebar, transparent dashboard overview area, individual metric cards, a framed tracker panel, familiar toolbar controls, and status-colored board columns. Future dashboard changes should preserve the approved card information structure: company, role, status, applied date, follow-up, contact, and actions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA fundamentals: readable contrast, visible focus states, semantic controls, keyboard-accessible actions, and 44px minimum touch targets on coarse-pointer devices. Avoid color-only status communication and respect reduced-motion preferences.
